## About the Declaration

The **International Moral–Economic Declaration for Global Peace, Food Justice, and the Continuity of All Living and Non-Living Beings** is an independently authored moral-policy instrument concerning peace, food justice, ethical economics, human dignity, ecological responsibility, and the continuity of living and non-living beings.

The document characterizes itself as:

- peaceful;
- non-binding;
- non-treaty;
- non-coercive;
- consultative and policy-oriented; and
- respectful of the sovereignty and equality of States.

Its stated ethical foundations include **Aram (Righteousness)**, **Ahimsa (Non-Violence)** and **Vasudhaiva Kutumbakam — “The World is One Family.”**

---

## Core Areas of Concern

The Declaration addresses the relationship between economic systems, peace, hunger, dignity and continuity.

Its principal areas of concern include:

- global peace and conflict prevention;
- food justice and the eradication of hunger;
- ethical and non-violent economics;
- poverty and inequality;
- responsible use of economic and monetary power;
- protection of living beings and ecological systems;
- continuity and dignity of living and non-living beings;
- peaceful international trade and cooperation; and
- intergenerational responsibility.

The document also proposes, for voluntary international consultation, exploration of an **Aram-based, peace-oriented economic framework**, including ethical symbolism associated with the Indian Rupee as a conceptual reference for peace-centred commerce.

## Moral–Economic Framework

The Declaration advances the proposition that economic systems should serve:

- peace rather than conflict;
- hunger eradication rather than deprivation;
- justice rather than domination;
- human dignity rather than exploitation; and
- the continuity of life rather than destructive economic practices.

It presents **Moral Economics** as an ethical and civilizational framework for international reflection rather than as an existing binding legal regime.

---

## Food Justice and the Hunger–Peace Nexus

The Declaration places particular emphasis on the relationship between hunger, deprivation, inequality and instability.

It presents food justice as an essential component of durable peace and invites international discussion concerning the reduction and eventual eradication of hunger.

These propositions are preserved as part of the author's moral-policy framework and should not be interpreted as independent findings of any governmental or international institution.

---

## Aram-Based Peace-Oriented Economic Concept

The publication proposes, for voluntary discussion, an **Aram-based peace-oriented economic instrument**.

This includes conceptual reflection on ethical symbolism associated with the Indian Rupee and its possible peace-oriented international identity.

The proposal is expressly framed as:

- voluntary;
- consultative;
- non-exclusive;
- non-discriminatory; and
- respectful of national monetary sovereignty.

It does not call for the abolition of existing currencies or mandatory international adoption of the Indian Rupee.

---

## Indian Rupee as a Conceptual “Peace Currency”

The Declaration explores the possible symbolic role of the Indian Rupee as a peace-oriented economic instrument.

Within the publication, this concept is associated with:

- moral symbolism;
- non-violence;
- compassion;
- universal dignity;
- life protection;
- ecological responsibility; and
- peace-centred trade.

This is an independently authored conceptual proposal.

It does not establish any official monetary policy of the Reserve Bank of India, the Government of India or any international financial institution.

## Implementation Roadmap

Annexure VII provides a structured, non-binding pathway for possible international discussion.

### Stage I — United Nations Discourse Initiation

Suggested areas of discussion include:

- moral economics;
- the hunger–peace nexus;
- ethical trade principles;
- academic and expert reflection; and
- civil-society participation.

### Stage II — Expert and Multi-Stakeholder Consultation

The document proposes possible dialogue involving:

- economists;
- ethicists;
- development experts;
- environmental scholars;
- food-security specialists; and
- civil-society organizations.

### Stage III — Moral Currency Concept Exploration

The publication proposes conceptual exploration of the Indian Rupee as a peace-oriented symbolic instrument.

The document expressly clarifies:

- no abolition of existing currencies;
- no mandatory global adoption; and
- no challenge to monetary sovereignty.

### Stage IV — Pilot and Voluntary Initiatives

Possible voluntary ideas include:

- ethical trade corridors;
- peace-labelled food supply chains;
- hunger-free pilot zones; and
- non-violent production certifications.

Participation is described as optional, transparent, time-bound and reversible.

### Stage V — Review, Feedback and Knowledge Sharing

The roadmap proposes:

- academic publications;
- informational reporting;
- public consultation;
- open knowledge sharing; and
- refinement of ethical-economic concepts.

No binding commitments are created by this roadmap.
