## About the Document

**Call for the Global Prohibition of Nuclear Weapons — A Moral Stand for Universal Peace (VSJP/INT/PEACE-007/2025)** is an independently authored peace and nuclear-disarmament appeal by **Aaryan Narasimhan Radhakrishnan**.

The publication presents a moral call for the elimination of nuclear weapons and for the advancement of disarmament through non-violence, peace, ecological responsibility and universal moral concern.

The underlying document describes itself as a universal moral declaration and addresses the continued existence, development, production, testing, storage and threat of nuclear weapons.

It is preserved in this GitHub repository as a historical and documentary public record.

---

## Central Purpose

The document advances the moral proposition that nuclear weapons pose a profound danger to humanity, other forms of life, ecological systems and the long-term continuity of civilization.

Its central orientation is toward:

- nuclear disarmament;
- prevention of nuclear destruction;
- non-violence;
- ecological responsibility;
- protection of life;
- peaceful international cooperation; and
- long-term civilizational continuity.

The document seeks a future in which international security is pursued through peaceful means rather than through the threat of catastrophic destruction.

## Moral Position on Nuclear Weapons

The underlying document characterizes the continued development and stockpiling of nuclear weapons as a moral failure of civilization.

It calls upon nations to move toward collective agreement concerning the prohibition of:

- nuclear-weapons production;
- nuclear-weapons storage;
- nuclear-weapons testing; and
- the threat or use of nuclear weapons.

The document further presents peace not as weakness, but as a higher form of responsibility and civilizational intelligence.

These statements are the author’s moral and policy position and should not be interpreted as a statement of existing universal legal obligations beyond applicable international law.

---

## Ecological and Humanitarian Concern

The document also addresses the ecological and humanitarian dimensions of nuclear-weapons systems.

Its concerns include the environmental implications of uranium extraction and the broader consequences of nuclear-weapons development for:

- soil;
- ecosystems;
- humanity;
- other forms of life;
- future generations; and
- planetary continuity.

The publication therefore frames nuclear disarmament not only as a security issue, but also as an ethical, ecological and humanitarian concern.

---

## Protection of Life and Continuity

The publication advances a broad moral concern for forms of existence that may be affected by nuclear destruction.

Its language extends moral consideration beyond humanity and reflects the author’s broader continuity-oriented ethical philosophy.

This framing should be understood as a philosophical and moral position.

It does not, by itself, create:

- legal personality for non-human or non-sentient entities;
- enforceable international rights;
- compulsory obligations upon States; or
- new rules of international law.

## Appeal to the International Community

The underlying document appeals to the United Nations, disarmament institutions and sovereign States to support movement toward the complete prohibition of nuclear weapons.

This appeal is non-binding and does not purport to compel any State or institution.

Any actual international disarmament measure would remain subject to:

- applicable international law;
- treaty processes;
- sovereign consent;
- competent institutional procedures; and
- the constitutional and legal systems of participating States.
