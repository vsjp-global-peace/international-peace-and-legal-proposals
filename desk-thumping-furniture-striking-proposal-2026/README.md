## About the Proposal
The **Good-Faith International Proposal for the Voluntary Cessation of Desk-Thumping and Furniture-Striking in Parliamentary and Legislative Deliberation** is an independently authored international ethical proposal concerning dignity, restraint, peaceful expression and the respectful use of institutional furniture within parliamentary and legislative deliberation.
The proposal respectfully invites parliaments, legislative assemblies and comparable deliberative institutions worldwide to consider the **voluntary discontinuation of intentional striking, pounding or thumping of desks, tables or institutional furniture as a form of deliberative expression**, where dignified non-impact alternatives are reasonably available.
Its central principle is:
> **Human expression does not require unnecessary physical impact upon an uninvolved object.**
The proposal seeks not silence, but conscious restraint.
---
## Core Ethical Principles
The instrument advances four principal ethical ideas:
- **Material Respect** — objects created for institutional service should be treated with reasonable care and restraint.
- **Purpose Integrity** — desks, tables and institutional furniture should primarily serve the purposes for which they were created.
- **Peaceful Deliberation** — democratic strength should arise from ideas, speech, argument, lawful disagreement and decision-making rather than unnecessary physical impact.
- **Acoustic Restraint** — avoidable impact-generated sound may be reduced where dignified alternatives are reasonably available.
---
## Scope
The proposal concerns the **intentional expressive striking of desks, tables or institutional furniture** during parliamentary, legislative or comparable deliberative proceedings.
It does not concern:
- ordinary or functional use of furniture;
- accidental contact;
- maintenance or repair;
- safety-related conduct;
- emergency action; or
- other legitimate institutional uses.
---
## Voluntary Non-Impact Alternatives
The proposal does not prescribe a single universal method of parliamentary or legislative expression.
Institutions may independently consider alternatives such as:
- applause;
- recognized verbal assent;
- electronic or visual acknowledgment; or
- other dignified non-impact methods consistent with their own constitutional, legal, procedural and institutional traditions.
No single model is proposed or required.
---
## The “Unheard Cry” — Ethical Metaphor
The proposal uses the concept of an **“Unheard Cry”** solely as an ethical metaphor encouraging restraint toward objects that cannot speak for themselves.
This metaphor does **not** assert that furniture or other inanimate objects possess:
- biological life;
- consciousness;
- sentience;
- pain perception;
- legal personality; or
- enforceable rights.
The metaphor serves ethical reflection only and does not create a scientific, biological or legal claim concerning inanimate objects.

Each parliament, legislature or comparable deliberative institution retains authority under its own constitutional, legal and procedural framework to determine whether and how the proposal should be considered.
The proposal may therefore be:

No uniform international parliamentary model is asserted.
