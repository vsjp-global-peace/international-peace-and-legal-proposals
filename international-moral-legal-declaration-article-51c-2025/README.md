## About the Declaration

The **International Moral–Legal Declaration under Article 51(c) of the Constitution of India** is an independently authored, conscience-based moral and legal appeal concerning international peace, life, dignity, ethical responsibility, and the continuity of living and non-living beings.

The publication is presented as:

- peaceful;
- conscience-based;
- non-adversarial;
- non-litigative;
- non-binding;
- non-coercive; and
- rooted in Aram, non-violence and ethical responsibility.

The Declaration does not seek sanctions, liability, compulsory enforcement or coercive legal consequences.

---

## Constitutional Context

The Declaration is framed in reference to **Article 51(c) of the Constitution of India**.

The document was submitted through the constitutional office of the President of India as part of the author's effort to place the moral and legal concerns expressed in the Declaration into public and institutional consideration.

The existence of a submission or registration reference does not, by itself, establish governmental endorsement, substantive acceptance, diplomatic transmission or adoption.

---

## Core Moral–Legal Concern

The Declaration advances a conscience-based appeal concerning the protection of:

- international peace;
- life;
- human dignity;
- ethical responsibility;
- non-violence;
- continuity of living beings; and
- continuity and responsible treatment of non-living beings and systems.

The work also raises an authorial moral concern regarding the adequacy of existing international structures in safeguarding peace, life, dignity and continuity.

Any such characterization represents the author's moral and legal position within the Declaration and should not be presented as an established finding of the United Nations or any other institution.

---

## Appeal Concerning the United Nations

The Declaration includes a moral appeal directed toward the United Nations.

It requests public reflection on what the author characterizes as moral incompleteness in the international protection of peace, life, dignity and continuity.

This appeal:

- is not an accusation of legal liability;
- does not seek litigation;
- does not seek sanctions;
- does not claim compulsory jurisdiction;
- does not create legal obligations; and
- does not represent a finding or admission by the United Nations.

It remains an independently authored moral–legal proposal for international reflection.
