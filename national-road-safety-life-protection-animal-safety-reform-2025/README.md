## About the Submission

The **National Road-Safety, Life-Protection & Animal-Safety Reform** is an independently authored constitutional policy submission concerning the protection of human life, animal life, public safety, road safety, technological accountability, and the continuity of all beings using Indian roads.

The submission is framed with reference to:

- **Article 21** of the Constitution of India;
- **Article 48A** of the Constitution of India;
- **Article 51A(g)** of the Constitution of India;
- road-safety regulation;
- environmental and wildlife protection;
- animal welfare;
- technological road-safety systems; and
- preventive public-safety measures.

The proposal seeks consideration of a coordinated road-safety framework addressing human safety, animal safety, driver responsibility, technological safeguards, enforcement, monitoring, and institutional accountability.

---

## Principal Reform Areas Presented in the Document

The submission proposes consideration of measures concerning:

1. strengthened enforcement of traffic rules;
2. animal-safe road corridors, crossings and fencing;
3. stricter driving-licence regulation;
4. a National Road-Safety Medical Protocol;
5. protection and safety measures for drivers and transport workers;
6. regulation concerning liquor outlets on highways;
7. AI/GPS-integrated driving-accountability mechanisms;
8. fast-track mechanisms for road-safety violations;
9. a National Animal-Accident Database;
10. constitutional and policy protection of human and animal life;
11. mandatory or progressively strengthened ADAS safety systems;
12. AI-assisted animal-detection technologies; and
13. vehicle black-box and related safety technologies.

These proposals are presented as policy recommendations for consideration and do not, by themselves, create legal obligations or governmental mandates.

## Constitutional and Public-Interest Character

The proposal was submitted as a peaceful and good-faith public-interest policy framework seeking consideration of measures intended to reduce preventable harm on roads and strengthen protection for human beings, animals and other vulnerable road users.

Its principal ethical premise is that road safety should be approached not only as traffic administration, but also as a matter of:

- protection of life;
- responsible use of technology;
- prevention of avoidable harm;
- humane treatment of animals;
- institutional accountability; and
- long-term public safety.
