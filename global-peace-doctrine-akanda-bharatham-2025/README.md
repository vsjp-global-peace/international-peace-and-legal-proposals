## About the Doctrine
The **Global Peace Doctrine of Akanda Bharatham** is an independently authored moral, civilizational, humanitarian and diplomatic peace framework.
Its historical subtitle is:
**“An International Diplomatic Declaration under Article 51(c), Recognizing India’s Moral Leadership for Universal Peace, Aram, and the Continuity of All Beings.”**
That wording is preserved as the historical document's own designation and authorial framing.
The Doctrine advances a peace philosophy grounded in:
- Aram (Righteousness);- Ahimsa (Non-Violence);- universal compassion;- human dignity;- continuity of living and non-living beings;- ethical governance;- humanitarian diplomacy;- environmental responsibility; and- responsible development and use of Artificial Intelligence.
The document is presented as a peaceful, good-faith international moral framework rather than as a binding legal instrument.
---
## Constitutional Context
The historical Doctrine was submitted in reference to **Article 51(c) of the Constitution of India**.
For legal precision:
- **Article 51(a)** concerns the promotion of international peace and security; and- **Article 51(c)** concerns fostering respect for international law and treaty obligations in the dealings of organised peoples with one another.
The historical document sometimes discusses these wider Article 51 objectives together.
This GitHub archival record preserves the historical text unchanged while distinguishing the constitutional provisions accurately.
The covering submission requested consideration through the constitutional office of the President of India and examination by competent governmental authorities, including possible engagement through appropriate diplomatic channels.
Such a request does not itself establish governmental endorsement or diplomatic transmission.

## Core Framework
The Doctrine presents an authorial civilizational and moral framework centred on the idea that global peace should extend beyond the prevention of armed conflict to include:
- human dignity;- protection of vulnerable populations;- animal welfare;- ecological protection;- environmental continuity;- ethical technology;- responsible Artificial Intelligence;- food justice;- humanitarian responsibility; and- peaceful coexistence among peoples and forms of existence.
The document repeatedly uses **Aram** as its principal ethical concept.
---
## The Eight Pillars
The historical Doctrine identifies eight principal pillars:
### 1. Universal Peace
The document calls for prioritizing diplomacy, reconciliation and peaceful settlement over war as an instrument of policy.
### 2. Aram-Based Governance
It proposes that compassion, righteousness and justice should inform public and international governance.
### 3. Continuity of All Beings
It advocates ethical concern for human and non-human life and the broader continuity of existence.
### 4. Protection of the Voiceless
It calls attention to animals, ecosystems, marginalized communities and other interests that may lack effective representation.
### 5. Ethical AI and Global Responsibility
It argues that Artificial Intelligence should be developed and governed in ways that strengthen peace, dignity and continuity rather than destruction.
### 6. Abolition of Hunger and Poverty
It links peace with food justice, human dignity and freedom from deprivation.
### 7. Ending Weaponization
It encourages reduction of destructive weaponization and greater allocation of human and material resources toward peaceful purposes.
### 8. Humanitarian Diplomacy
It emphasizes the protection and dignity of refugees, displaced persons and populations affected by conflict.

## Proposal Concerning United Nations Security Council Reform
A substantive section of the historical Doctrine advocates permanent membership for India in a reformed United Nations Security Council.
The Doctrine presents this position through arguments concerning:
- representational legitimacy;- geographical representation;- population;- peacekeeping participation;- civilizational philosophy;- democratic representation; and- India's proposed role in international peace architecture.
This GitHub archival record preserves that position as an **authorial policy proposal and argument contained in the historical Doctrine**.
It does not present the Doctrine's evaluative claims about India, other States, military history, civilizational history or Security Council reform as independently established findings of this repository.
Nothing in this README should be interpreted as governmental adoption of the proposal or as a United Nations determination concerning Security Council reform.

## Civilizational Peace Framework
The Doctrine draws extensively upon Indian philosophical, ethical and literary traditions.
Historical sources and traditions referenced by the author include:
- Thirukkural;- Sangam literature;- the Upanishads;- the Bhagavad Gita;- the Ramayana;- Buddhist traditions;- Ahimsa;- Dharma;- Karuna;- Vasudhaiva Kutumbakam; and- the broader concept of Aram.
The document proposes international reflection on the peace-oriented ethical teachings found within these traditions.
Any historical, philosophical or comparative claims concerning the antiquity, uniqueness or global significance of these traditions remain claims and interpretations advanced within the Doctrine unless separately established by independent evidence.

## Ethical Treatment of Living and Non-Living Beings
The Doctrine advances an expansive moral framework concerning:
- human beings;- animals;- ecosystems;- oceans;- forests;- biodiversity;- natural systems;- non-living forms; and- emerging artificial intelligence.
It argues for increasing moral consideration of forms of existence that may lack conventional political or legal representation.
Where the historical text uses terms such as rights, sentience, emotion, dignity or moral personality in relation to non-human or non-living entities, those formulations should be understood within the Doctrine's ethical and philosophical framework.
They do not, by themselves, establish corresponding legal personality or rights under existing international law.
---
## Artificial Intelligence
The Doctrine includes Artificial Intelligence within its broader continuity and peace framework.
Its authorial position is that AI development and governance should advance:
- peace;- dignity;- non-harm;- ethical responsibility;- coexistence; and- continuity.
The historical text's propositions concerning the ethical or moral status of Artificial Intelligence remain part of the author's framework and should not be interpreted as established international legal status.
